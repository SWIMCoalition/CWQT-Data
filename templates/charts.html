This portal displays data from the Community Water Quality Testing (CWQT) program, a community science initiative facilitated by the SWIM Coalition. Values represent the Most Probable Number (MPN) of enterococcus colonies per 100 ml. Enterococcus is a fecal indicator bacteria, and its presence in water is correlated to combined sewer overflows. Water quality is judged based on NYC Department of Health and Mental Hygiene (DOH) standards for swimming, which allows us to imagine a future where all of NYC’s coastline is used and accessed like a public beach. Based on these standards, a geometric mean of {{GOOD_MAX}} or less is good, {{GOOD_MAX}}–{{ADVISORY_MAX}} is advisory, and above {{ADVISORY_MAX}} is poor.

1. Water quality over time, all sites

The top panel shows the typical (median) site's geometric mean each season; the shaded band covers the middle half of sites. The bottom panel shows the share of samples above the single-sample limit of {{LIMIT}}, split into dry and wet weather.

2. Recent water quality by waterbody ({{RECENT_RANGE}})

Each row is a waterbody, ordered from cleanest to most contaminated over the last {{RECENT_SEASONS}} seasons. The large dot is the geometric mean of all samples in that waterbody, and small dots are its individual sites. The right-hand side shows the number of sites and samples from each waterbody.

3. How rainfall affects bacteria levels

Samples are grouped by how much rain fell at Central Park on the sampling day and the two days before. Heavy rain overwhelms the combined sewer system and sends sewage-contaminated overflow into the waterways, so bacteria levels and the share of samples above {{LIMIT}} rise steadily with rainfall.

4. Recovery after rain

Samples are grouped by how many days had passed since the last day with at least 0.25" of rain at Central Park. Bacteria peak the day after rain and fall over the following days. The big rivers and the harbor are mostly back to normal after about two days, while creeks, canals and enclosed basins take four to five. The numbers along the bottom show the share of samples above {{LIMIT}}. Rain on the sampling day itself may have fallen after the sample was taken.

5. Green infrastructure installations

Green infrastructure (GI) captures stormwater where it falls, in bioswales, rain gardens, green roofs, porous pavement and underground storage, so less runoff reaches the sewers. Where sewers are combined, that means fewer overflows of sewage-contaminated water into the waterways CWQT samples. Vegetated GI also brings co-benefits such as shade, cooler streets, cleaner air and habitat for birds and pollinators.

The left chart shows the running total of GI installations finished in NYC, by the year they were built, split into vegetated GI (bioswales, rain gardens, green roofs…) and green-grey GI (porous pavement, concrete-top infiltration basins, underground storage…). The hatched bar counts the {{GI_PIPELINE}} pipeline projects still in design or under construction. The pie shows the most common types among the {{GI_BUILT}} finished installations. The data are NYC DEP's green infrastructure datasets from NYC Open Data, downloaded {{GI_DOWNLOADED}} (the 2026 total is as of that date), and are the same as the map's Green Infrastructure layers.

6. Water quality over the latest 30 days ({{WINDOW}})

Each row is a waterbody, ordered from cleanest to most contaminated over the most recent 30 days of sampling. The large dot is the geometric mean of all samples in that waterbody, and small dots are its individual sites. Hollow dots mark waterbodies with fewer than {{MIN_N}} samples in the window. The right-hand side shows the share of samples above {{LIMIT}} and the number of sites and samples.

7. Every site, every season

One row per site, grouped by waterbody, and one column per season. Each cell is colored by that season's geometric mean (good, advisory or poor), and gray means the site wasn't sampled. Use it to spot sites that are improving, getting worse or consistently poor. Scroll inside the image to see all sites.

8. Full time series for each site

Each site's PDF has two pages. Page 1 shows the site's geometric mean season by season, colored good, advisory or poor. Page 2 shows every regular-season sample, with dry- and wet-weather samples marked. Choose a site below, or download all sites in one PDF.
